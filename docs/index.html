The real thing

An hour of real US traffic.

Every airliner broadcasts its vertical rate about once a second. Horus reads it from public ADS-B data (the adsb.lol archive and OpenSky), scales it by what each aircraft type normally shows in smooth air, forecasts the next 15 minutes on a 3D grid, and checks itself against pilot reports it never sees.

    What Horus is saying

      Real data, not a mock-up: every dot is an aircraft that was above FL200 over the US on 1 January 2026, 15:00–16:00 UTC, replayed through the same code as the live loop. Detections appear at the minute they happened; hover a pulse or a triangle for details.

      Research prototype. Not for flight decisions or any operational use.

      How it works

      From a jittery number to a forecast.

      The chart is real: two Boeing jets of the same type at a similar level, the same half hour. One flew through rough air. Switch between them.

      Vertical rate, then the per-minute index

      ft/min as broadcast · index = roughness ÷ the type's smooth-air scale

      Read the broadcast

      Vertical rate, altitude and position from public ADS-B. Rounded to 64 ft/min, and the archive only stores changes of 200 ft/min or more.

      Keep level flight

      Climbs and descents are cut out. Over 120 s windows: above FL200, within 300 ft, median rate under 200 ft/min.

      Measure what's left

      Remove the slow trend, correct the rounding noise (Sheppard), and divide by what this aircraft type shows in smooth air.

      Alarm, forecast, check

      An index at or above the threshold is a detection. It lights its ~45 km cell; the odds fade with a fitted half-life and drift with GFS winds. Pilot reports score it.

      The architecture

      A sensor network made of airliners.

      One pure core runs both the offline benchmark and the live loop. Everything else feeds it or measures it.

      Data ingestion

      3.5GB

      streamed per archive day and never written to disk. Only continental-US traffic at FL180 and above is kept (about 220 MB a day), as typed, unit-checked Parquet.

      $ make up

      One command installs the locked environment and fetches only what is missing: the archive day, pilot reports, aircraft types and GFS winds.

      Spatial mapping

      ~45km

      hexagonal forecast cells, 2,000 ft deep: 8,388 cells × 13 flight levels over the continental US.

      Detection

      σ/ type

      Level flight only. Trend removed, ADS-B's 64 ft/min rounding corrected, divided by the aircraft type's smooth-air scale.

      Forecast

      50.3min

      half-life of a detection at cruise, fitted from data (11.6 min lower down). The odds drift with GFS upper winds.

      Efficiency

      32.3ms

      to update the whole grid for 10,000 aircraft (p95) on an 8 GB M1. Python and NumPy, a uv lockfile, no Docker.

      Offline equals live

      Bit-identical.

      A test replays a recorded half hour of real traffic through both the live loop and the offline pipeline, and requires the same grid, to the last bit.

      Engineering rigour

      Nothing leaks.

      Fitted on December, scored on January. A leakage test fails if a test date reaches any fitted artefact, and every modelling choice has a benchmark row.

      Limitation

      At a strict false-alarm setting, the engine isolates high-confidence turbulence but currently captures a low overall percentage of reported events.

      0.2%of reported turbulence caught at 1 false alarm per 1,000 flight-hours
      5.7%at 10 false alarms per 1,000 flight-hours

      Why: most turbulence never gets a pilot report, so every "false alarm" may be real but unreported, and the public archive drops the small jitter that light chop leaves in the data.

      Weak labels

      Pilot reports are sparse, late and roughly placed, and most turbulence is never reported, so false-alarm rates are upper bounds.

      Coarse public data

      The archive stores a new vertical rate only when it moves 200 ft/min or more (plus a ~20 s heartbeat). Smaller jitter is lost.

      Replay, not live

      Free live feeds give about one point per aircraft every 90 s, too sparse to detect, so the live loop runs on an archived day.

      One winter, one region

      44 days of data, continental US, FL200 to FL460.

      Roughness, not EDR

      Measured in ft/min per aircraft type; not calibrated to eddy dissipation rate, the aviation standard.

      Negative evidence hurt

      Lowering the odds after smooth passes made the forecast worse (ROC-AUC 0.471), so it stays off.

      Run it

      Three commands.

      macOS or Linux with uv. Public data only, no account or API key. The first make up streams about 3.5 GB.

      Terminal
      $ make up      # environment + the demo day's data
$ make watch   # live text stream: an archived afternoon at 30x
$ make cases   # three case studies, minute by minute
